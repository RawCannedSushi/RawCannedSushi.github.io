Proper Playing

What is "proper"?

I understand what I am about to say may seem like this page is a waste of time, but in reality there is no "correct" way to play a record. You can play it on whatever you like, with whatever needle you like, who cares. There are only ways to ensure the longevity of your records, maximize sound quality, and minimize interference. On this page I will lightly brush over some basics then go over signal chains.

All About Needles

Everything we are about to brush over has to do with the needle of the record player so I figured I'd put it under this category.

Speaker Placement

Speaker placement is everything when it comes to a turntable setup, and is the easiest and cheapest upgrade you can do to an existing one. Speakers should be at ear level, at least 2 feet from the wall, and pointing in their respective stereo directions. The biggest issue with this though is that speakers should be placed on a seperate surface from the turntable. The vibrations caused by the speakers if they are on the same surface as the turntable can make the needle vibrate, skip, or even bounce. These can all worsen audio quality, damage the needle, and damage the record.

Needle Pressure/Tracking Force

Referenced on the turntables page, some turntables have an adjustable counterweight or other methods in which the tracking force of a needle can be adjusted. The tracking force is essentially the amount of pressure the needle applies while playing on the record. Proper tracking force differs from needle to needle, so its best to simply search up what the proper tracking force for your needle is. To adjust the tracking force to the proper weight, you will need a record stylus tracking force scale which can be bought for just a couple of bucks off of Amazon. Tracking force is important because a tracking force that is too high can cause a scraping sound when playing, and damage the record or needle very easily. On the other hand, a too light tracking force causes the needle to essentially get thrown around within the grooves of the record. This can cause the needle to tear up the sides of the record or even skip easily.

The Signal Chain

Here we enter possible the most complicated thing on this whole site: The Signal Chain. What the signal chain is is the way the audio data travels from your record player to the speakers and the steps it makes along the way. It can differ depending on what you're doing (bluetooth, writing to PC, built in pre-amp) but it generally looks the same for 99% of people. Below is a chart of the average signal chain. The top being where it starts, and the bottom where it ends.

Turntable - Captures the audio signal from the record using needle. Initially very quiet.

Pre-Amp - Sometimes built into the turntable, a Pre-Amp amplifies the initial quiet signal from the turntable in order to make it usable by the speaker-amp/speakers.

Speaker Amp - Sometimes built into the speakers (known as "Active Speakers"), a speaker amp takes the signal from the pre-amp and amplifies it to a level that can be output by the speakers.

Speakers - The final step in the signal chain, speakers take the signal from the speaker amp and output it as sound.
