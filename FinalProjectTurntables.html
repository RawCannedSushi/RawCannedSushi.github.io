Turntables

A Little Backstory

The beating heart of any record playing setup, room, or experience is the turntable. Almost the entire experience of playing a vinyl record occurs as the turntable- taking the record out of its sleeve, placing it on the spindle, lifting the needle, and hearing that slight crackle as music starts to first play out of your speakers. But sadly, some people like to treat the hobby as less accessible than it really is.

“Vinyl Elitists” as I like to call them, or just audiophiles who don't realize not everyone has the budget or the trained ears to hear the miniscule differences in quality like them, have brought a negative stigma to the space, especially surrounding turntable choice. These people a lot of the time will harp that “You’re missing out” or “You’re destroying your records!” if you spend less than half a grand on a record player. I’m here to tell you that they’re flat wrong. There are plenty of budget options now-a-days that provide a high quality and safe listening experience to the average listener. And while myself as a pretty big audiophile likes to not associate with those people, there is sadly one point almost anyone seriously into collecting vinyl records can agree on: the most popular player in the world actually does destroy records.

The Suitcase Scandal

Suitcase players, commonly referred to generally as the brand name “Crosleys”, are some of the most common types of record players you’ll see someone own. It is unfortunate that these are peddled by many brands, mostly Crosley and Victrola, as a high quality record player. The flaws within these dirt cheap players actually make your records lose value the second they rest on the platter. I could go on for days about issues with these but I’ll keep it quick. Starting with the needle, these record players are typically packaged with a sapphire or ruby needle rather than the standard diamond. These softer needles tend to scratch up records and go bad at an exponentially faster rate than diamond tipped needles. The price difference between sapphire and diamond needles isn't even that large- a replacement crosley needle costs ~$20 while a basic diamond one will set you back ~$30. In fact, it’s probably CHEAPER to buy the diamond one as it wont wear down nearly as fast. Next is the platter. These turntables don't have a full 12 inch platter for the record to lay on. The record hanging off + gravity + the force of the tonearm can cause records to warp quickly, especially if you leave one on the platter for an extended time. Speaking of the force of the tonearm, let's talk about that. The force of the tonearm (the arm that the needle is on) is all out of whack and adds to the scratching power of these record players. The final point I'm going to bring up today is built in speakers. Speakers built into a turntable can cause the tonearm to vibrate or even bounce/skip which will scratch the record or damage the needle if you are even the slightest bit unlucky. Though, I may have a lot of strong words for the most popular budget record player, that does not mean there are not good budget record players.

A Beginners Guide

Now time for what this is actually for, the beginners guide to turntables. Aside from the avoid suitcase players rule I am going to attach 3 tables below this paragraph that go over three things: A what to avoid in record players table, best budget tables $100-200, and mid-range tables for beginners under $500. None of these will have speakers built in so you will need to provide your own. If you don't own a speaker amplifier or speakers already, make sure to do some research or just get “active” speakers that do not require a separate amplifier. All of the listed turntables will have a built-in phono pre-amp. The phono pre-amp is the component that takes the quiet sound from the needle and amplifies it to be sent to the speaker amplifier or speakers themselves. A lot of people who are into vinyl like to have separate pre-amps not built into the turntable, but since this is a beginners guide and technology is a lot better now-a-days, I will be keeping it simple with them being built in.
